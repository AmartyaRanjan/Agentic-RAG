# Stage-2 Experimental Results — 2-DOF Reacher and LEAP Hand

**Date:** 16 September 2026
**Platform:** NVIDIA L4 (23 GB), Isaac Sim 5.1.0, Isaac Lab 2.3.0 (`3c6e67bb5`), rl_games 1.6.1, PyTorch 2.7.0+cu128
**Code:** `isaac_env/pi_adr/` · **Full engineering log:** `isaac_env/pi_adr/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md`

## 1. What was built

No 2-DOF benchmark existed in the codebase, so it was implemented from the stage-1 report's
equations as an Isaac Lab `DirectRLEnv` and validated against them before use.

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Links / masses | 0.5 m, 0.5 m / 1.0 kg, 0.5 kg |
| Joints | 2 revolute about Z, continuous (see §5) |
| Actuation | torque, `τ = 20·clip(a,−1,1)` Nm, ‖q̇‖ ≤ 5 rad/s, joint damping 0.05 |
| Observation / action | 8-D `[q₁,q₂,q̇₁,q̇₂,x_t,y_t,x_t−x_ee,y_t−y_ee]` / 2-D |
| Rates | 120 Hz physics, 30 Hz control (decimation 4), 10 s episodes = 300 steps |
| Targets | `r ~ U(0.20, 0.85)`, `θ ~ U(0, 2π)`, success radius 5 cm |
| Parallelism | 4096 environments |
| Rewards | `R_vanilla = −d + 5·I(d<0.05) − 0.005‖a‖²`; `R_eureka = e^(−10d) + 100·I(d<0.05) − 0.01‖q̇‖² − 0.1` |

PPO hyperparameters follow the report exactly (8→256→128→64 ELU, lr 1e-3 adaptive, γ 0.99,
GAE λ 0.95, clip 0.2, entropy 0.005, desired KL 0.01, 8 epochs, 4 minibatches, horizon 24).

## 2. Headline results — 2-DOF reaching

All rows use one frozen metric definition (`METRICS_VERSION 1.0.0`), one evaluation protocol
(4096 environments, deterministic actions, fixed seed 1234, terminal error at t = 9.93 s), so
every number is directly comparable.

| Run | <5 cm | <2 cm | <1 cm | <5 mm | <1 mm | mean (mm) | median (mm) | p90 (mm) | mean q̇ (rad/s) | effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **IK+PD reference** (not learned) | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% | 100% | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.155 | 0.013 |
| Vanilla PPO @5000 | 93.04% | 13.28% | 2.22% | 0.34% | 0.02% | 101.9 | 34.7 | 44.3 | 1.534 | 0.514 |
| Eureka PPO @500 | 96.97% | 25.56% | 3.59% | 0.78% | 0.05% | 46.4 | 28.5 | 43.9 | 3.396 | 1.436 |
| Eureka PPO @1500 | 99.05% | 26.37% | 7.91% | 1.49% | 0.05% | 38.6 | 30.1 | 41.5 | 2.522 | 1.073 |

### Comparison with the stage-1 report

| Metric | Report (Eureka 500) | **Ours (Eureka 500)** | Report (Eureka ~1500) | **Ours (Eureka 1500)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| <5 cm | 97.90% | **96.97%** | 95.3% | **99.05%** |
| <2 cm | 19.02% | **25.56%** | 18.1% | **26.37%** |
| <1 cm | 3.56% | **3.59%** | 5.3% | **7.91%** |
| mean error | 33.5 mm | **46.4 mm** | 45.5 mm | **38.6 mm** |
| median error | 34.6 mm | **28.5 mm** | 33.3 mm | **30.1 mm** |

**The report's central finding reproduces.** The Eureka reward reaches the 5 cm region quickly
and then stops improving: success collapses from ~97% at 5 cm to ~26% at 2 cm and <8% at 1 cm.
The measured mechanism is the one the report proposes — the +100 success bonus dwarfs the
residual `e^(−10d)` shaping term once inside 5 cm, so there is no economic reason to refine
centimetres into millimetres.

### Where our results differ from the report

The report claims the human reward reaches 98.36% at 2 cm and 79.74% at 1 cm. **We do not
reproduce this.** Our vanilla policy reaches 13.28% at 2 cm and 2.22% at 1 cm, and is *worse*
than the Eureka reward at every threshold. Two measured reasons:

1. The vanilla reward's precision incentive is also weak. Going from 3 cm to 0.5 cm earns
   ≈0.025/step ≈ 7.5 reward against an episode return of ~1300 — about 0.6%. The +5 bonus
   dominates in the same way Eureka's +100 does, only less extremely.
2. Action σ collapses from 1.0 to ≈0.024 during training (entropy −4.66), against the report's
   stated late-training σ of 0.10–0.11. Exploration dies long before the fine-precision
   refinement can be found.

We therefore cannot confirm that the human reward is precision-friendly on this benchmark; on
our measurements both rewards are coarse-reaching rewards, and the discovered one is better.

## 3. The environment permits millimetre precision — the reward is what limits it

The single most important control: a scripted inverse-kinematics + PD controller, driven through
the *same* action interface (normalised → 20 Nm) and measured with the *same* metric suite.

| | <5 cm | <1 cm | <1 mm | median error | mean q̇ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IK + PD reference | 100% | 100% | 100% | 0.0 mm | 0.155 rad/s |

An optimal controller achieves **100% success at every threshold down to 1 mm**. Millimetre
accuracy is therefore fully attainable at 30 Hz with ±20 Nm — the learned policies' ~30 mm floor
is a property of the reward and the optimiser, not of the robot or the control rate. This makes
the reward comparison meaningful rather than a measurement of an environment ceiling.

## 4. Robustness under domain randomisation

Static per-environment DR, sampled once per episode. "In-distribution" lies inside the RAPP
envelope; "shifted" is a strictly harder, unseen distribution (roadmap §22).

| Policy | Physics | <5 cm | <2 cm | <1 cm | mean (mm) | median (mm) | mean q̇ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Eureka @1500 | nominal | 99.05% | 26.37% | 7.91% | 38.6 | 30.1 | 2.522 |
| Eureka @1500 | in-distribution DR | 97.88% | 34.89% | 9.30% | 45.9 | 25.1 | 1.280 |
| Eureka @1500 | shifted DR | 91.14% | **42.36%** | **11.47%** | 107.0 | **22.4** | 0.951 |
| Vanilla @5000 | nominal | 93.04% | 13.28% | 2.22% | 101.9 | 34.7 | 1.534 |
| Vanilla @5000 | in-distribution DR | 92.14% | 12.99% | 2.73% | 108.8 | 33.6 | 0.745 |
| Vanilla @5000 | shifted DR | 80.69% | 13.18% | 2.64% | 194.8 | 35.2 | 1.169 |

**A result worth highlighting:** for the Eureka policy, harder physics *degrades* coarse success
(99.05% → 91.14% at 5 cm) while *improving* fine precision (26.4% → 42.4% at 2 cm; median
30.1 → 22.4 mm). Friction, damping and weaker actuators impose the slowness that the reward
never asked for — mean joint velocity falls 2.52 → 0.95 rad/s. This is independent evidence for
the report's thesis: the precision gap is an objective-design problem, not a capability problem.
The vanilla policy, already slower, shows no such improvement.

## 5. RAPP — reward-aware physics prior

One-parameter-at-a-time feasibility sweep (DrEureka's protocol) on the nominal Eureka policy;
feasible = P(d<5 cm) ≥ 0.90, 1024 environments per candidate.

**C_RAPP = friction [0, 4.0] × damping_scale [0.25, 256] × effectiveness [0.3, 1.0] × mass_scale [1.0, 4.0]**

Selected rows from the physics–performance map:

| Parameter | value | S(π,ξ) | mean error (mm) | P(d<1 cm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| friction | 0.0 (nominal) | 0.974 | 45.0 | 3.61% |
| friction | 4.0 | 0.946 | 59.3 | 7.13% |
| friction | 8.0 | 0.843 | 94.6 | — infeasible |
| damping ×1 | nominal | 0.969 | 49.3 | 4.59% |
| damping ×128 | | 0.921 | 82.8 | **16.21%** |
| effectiveness | 0.3 | 0.915 | 53.4 | **16.02%** |
| effectiveness | 1.0 (nominal) | 0.972 | 47.1 | 8.11% |
| mass ×0.75 | | 0.786 | 57.9 | — infeasible |
| mass ×1.0 (nominal) | | 0.975 | 46.8 | — |
| mass ×4.0 | | 0.919 | 42.0 | — |

Two findings:

- **The feasible envelope is very wide.** The policy tolerates friction up to 4 Nm, damping up to
  256× nominal and actuators at 30% strength. Performance is nearly flat across most of it. The
  roadmap anticipates precisely this case: if RAPP already covers a region where performance is
  flat, adaptive DR has little left to exploit. That should be tested before the adaptive
  controller is justified.
- **The binding constraint is asymmetric in mass.** The policy fails with *lighter* links
  (≤0.75×) but tolerates 4× heavier. Lower inertia means more acceleration per unit torque, so a
  policy tuned to nominal inertia overshoots. Again consistent with the precision mechanism above.

## 6. Physics-informed observer (PINN)

A set-encoder deep ensemble (5 members) infers ξ = [m₁, m₂, f₁, f₂, b₁, b₂, α₁, α₂] from windows
of (q, q̇, τ, q̈). Actuator effectiveness is kept on the right-hand side of the EOM
(`M q̈ + C + F = α·τ_cmd`) with commanded torque as input, otherwise α is unidentifiable by
construction. The 2-link dynamics are in closed form and were verified against PhysX
(2.27% mean relative error on q̈; 0.036 Nm on recovered torque), so the physics residual is exact.

Validation splits are by **physics configuration**, never by time samples from a configuration
already seen.

| | acceleration RMSE | parameter error (normalised) | uncertainty ↔ error rank corr. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interpolation (held-out physics, same ranges) | 2.79 rad/s² | **0.065** | +0.11 |
| Extrapolation (ranges outside training) | 12.28 rad/s² | 0.209 | +0.10 |

Per-parameter absolute error (interpolation): m₁ 0.179 kg, m₂ 0.051 kg, f₁ 0.095, f₂ 0.114,
b₁ 0.042, b₂ 0.017, α₁ 0.064, α₂ 0.052.

**Negative result that matters for the roadmap.** The ensemble's predictive variance is only
weakly related to its actual error (rank correlation +0.11). The proposed adaptive-DR priority
score `w(ξ) = α_u·U_PINN(ξ) + α_f·[1−S(π,ξ)] + α_s·‖∇_ξ J‖` gives U_PINN a term of its own; on
this evidence that term would be close to noise. Either the uncertainty must be calibrated first,
or α_u should be set to zero and the claim dropped. Extrapolation degrading by 3× is the expected
and honest outcome, and supports the rule that adaptive DR stays inside C_RAPP.

## 8. Environment defects found and corrected

These are recorded because each one would have silently invalidated results.

| # | Defect | Effect | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Evaluation recorded the terminal distance *after* the environment's auto-reset | A trained policy scored 0.39% at 5 cm instead of 93% | Fixed (roll out `horizon−2`) |
| 2 | `ImplicitActuatorCfg(damping=0.0)` overrode the URDF's 0.05 | Nominal physics ≠ DR at scale 1.0; every DR comparison would carry an offset | Fixed |
| 3 | Hard ±π joint limits create a wrap-around trap | **Caps any controller at ~69% at 5 cm**; IK+PD failed 31.4% of episodes and 100% of those failures ended stalled against a joint stop | Fixed (continuous joints, wrapped observations) |
| 4 | PINN labels differenced over a control step, and the velocity limit engages from ~4 rad/s not 5 | Ground-truth ξ gave a *worse* acceleration RMSE (78.8) than the learned model — it was fitting label noise | Fixed (physics-step differencing, mask at 3.5 rad/s); true-ξ RMSE now 6.69 |
| 5 | Mass randomisation skipped the write when scale = 1.0 | RAPP's mass sweep inherited the previous candidate's masses; nominal scored 0.816 instead of 0.975 | Fixed |

Defect 3 also explains the report's 99.49% at 5 cm being unreachable for us initially: under hard
±π limits that number is not attainable by any controller, so the report's limits cannot have
been binding.

is not used as one).
